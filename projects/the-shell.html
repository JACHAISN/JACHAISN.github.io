Project 01

The Shell

A desert box turtle observation station in the Rio Grande bosque

Studio
ARCH 2315, Fall 2026
Program
Eco-Pavilion: Bosque Observation Station
Site
Rio Grande bosque, between Tingley Beach and the Rio Grande Nature Center
Tools
Rhino, Illustrator, InDesign, Photoshop, Blender

The desert box turtle lives in the bottom six inches of air and the top six feet of soil. A building standing on the ground looks down on all of this. The Shell cuts into the ground instead. The shell is broken apart into three sections, sitting around a court sunk three feet into the bosque floor: an observation room, an education room for ten visitors, and a study for one ecologist. Each shell meets the ground with a four-foot band. In the observation and education rooms the band is glass, so a visitor's eye is a foot above the leaf litter. In the study, it is a slatted screen. A glass panel drops behind it at night and stays raised for ventilation. Visitors leave the trail on the east and walk down a slope into the court. Every room opens onto it through a glass wall with a doorway at its middle. A ramp climbs to a deck four feet above the ground. The deck carries ten people above the observation room without a footstep on the burrowing ground, and its shade and the mist sprinklers beneath it keep the ground damp, the refuge the turtle uses at midday.
